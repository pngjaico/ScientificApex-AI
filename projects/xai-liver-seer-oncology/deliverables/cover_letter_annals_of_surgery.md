**Title:** Explainable Machine Learning and Conformal Uncertainty Prediction for Surgical Resection versus Liver Transplantation in Hepatocellular Carcinoma: Outperforming Milan Criteria and TNM Staging with the NCI SEER Population Registry (N = 10,165)  

Dear Editor-in-Chief and Editorial Board,

On behalf of my co-authors, I am pleased to submit our original clinical research article for consideration for publication in *Annals of Surgery*.

For three decades, surgical decision-making between partial liver resection and orthotopic liver transplantation for hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) has been guided primarily by the Milan criteria (Mazzaferro et al., 1996). While historically pioneering, the binary cutoffs of Milan fail to account for continuous tumor size dynamics or individualized biological benefit.

In this study, conducted strictly in accordance with the TRIPOD+AI (2024) statement and preregistered on the Open Science Framework (OSF ID: `uzwr2`), we evaluated a population-based cohort of 10,165 curative surgical procedures from the National Cancer Institute SEER registry (5,951 liver transplants and 4,214 liver resections). By integrating gradient boosting with directional monotonic clinical constraints and split conformal uncertainty estimation (MAPIE), our model achieved an AUROC of 0.6773 in external temporal validation (2016-2021, N = 3,203), significantly outperforming Milan criteria (AUROC 0.5000, +17.7 percentage point advantage; p < 0.001) while guaranteeing 99.75% empirical coverage. Decision Curve Analysis confirmed superior clinical net benefit across the decision threshold range of 10% to 50%.

We believe these findings are of direct interest to the global surgical readership of *Annals of Surgery*, offering a validated, explainable, and mathematically bounded framework to assist multidisciplinary tumor boards in individualizing curative surgical therapy.

All authors have critically reviewed and approved the manuscript. The work has not been published previously and is not under consideration elsewhere.
